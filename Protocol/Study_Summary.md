# Study Summary

## Protocol Details
Study Title: Breast and endometrial safety of micronised progesterone versus norethisterone acetate in menopausal hormone therapy (PROBES): study protocol of a double-blind randomized controlled trial
Protocol Number: Not stated
Protocol Version 3.1
ClinicalTrials.gov Identifier: NCT05586724
Condition being studied: Menopausal symptoms in naturally postmenopausal women.
Study Treatment Duration: 12 Months


## Study Objective
The protocol provides a injunction for a double-blind, randomized controlled trial that aims to asses the relative effectiveness of micronised progesterone (mP) with norethisterone acetate (NETA), a synthetic progestogen,  in continuous combination with oral oestrogen for the purpose of menopausal hormone therapy (HRT). Established data suggest that mP has better safety profile for breasts along with lower endometrium efficacy as compared to its synthetic counterpart. However, comparative randomised trial data are lacking, which this study aims to provide.

## Study Background
Menopause is a stage of reproductive life where menstrual periods stop permanently. It is not identified as a disease or disorder but inhibits oestrogen production and lead to a range of climacteric symptoms such as hot flashes, night sweats, vaginal dryness and most critically, reduce bone density. These symptoms can be clinically managed via oestrogen therapy. However, oestrogn can also cause excessive thickening of the endometrium lining (endometrial hyperplasia), which significantly increases the risk of endometrial cancer. Progesterone is often combined with oestrogen therapy to limit excessive cell proliferation, promote shedding of the endometrial lining, convert the proliferative endometrium into a secretory endometrium. In addition to their effects on the endometrium, progestogens exert biological effects on breast tissue, often associated with breast cancer and similar issues. Thus, breast and endometrial safety becomes important consideration in menopausal HRT.

## Intervention & Comparator
The study is divides into two parts:
Part 1: A double-blind, randomised controlled trial comparing 12 months of treatment where **intervention** is 100 mg mP + 1 mg oestradiol and the **comparator** is 0.5 mg NETA + 1 mg oestradiol along with a matched placebo. The primary objective is to compare the treatments based on the percentage change in mammographic breast density after 12 months.
Part 2: An open-label, single-arm study evaluating the endometrial safety of 100 mg mP + 1 mg oestradiol over 12 months by assessing the incidence of endometrial hyperplasia and endometrial cancer.

## Study Design
Phase is not stated
Multicenter
Sectioned in 2 parts:
Part 1 - Double-blind, Randomised, Controlled, Parallel-group, Two treatment arms, 1:1 allocation, 260 participants (130 per arm)
Part 2 - Open-label, Single arm, 260 participants

## Endpoint
### Primary Endpoint
Part 1 - Percentage change in mammographic breast density after 12 months
Part 2 - Incidence of endometrial pathology (hyperplasia and endometrial cancer) after 12 months.
### Secondary Endpoint
Part 1 - Breast cell proliferation, Endometrial histology and proliferation, Endometrial thickness, Bleeding pattern, Vaginal and gut microbiome, Serum hormone and metabolic as well as lipid and coagulation markers, Depression and anxiety scores, Health-related quality of life, Menopausal symptom scores
Part 2 - No secondary objectives are explicitly specified in the protocol.

## Study Population
### Inclusion Criteria
Healthy naturally postmenopausal women
Age 45–60 years
Menopausal symptoms affecting quality of life
BMI >19 to ≤32 kg/m²
Intact uterus
Required washout from previous menopausal hormone therapy
Written informed consent
### Exclusion Criteria
History or risk of breast or endometrial cancer
Abnormal baseline mammogram or endometrial biopsy
Vaginal bleeding
Significant cardiovascular, liver or psychiatric disease
Current menopausal hormone therapy
Alcohol or drug abuse
Hypersensitivity to study medication
